2. Explain any one of the following:

(a) Civilization, in the real sense of the term, consists not in the multiplication, but in the deliberate and voluntary restriction of wants.
Answer:
This line is taken from Mahatma Gandhi's renowned essay, "Indian Civilization and Culture." In this statement, Gandhi highlights the fundamental difference between modern Western civilization and ancient Indian civilization. He argues that true civilization is not about endlessly multiplying our desires and relentlessly chasing material comforts, which leads to greed and dissatisfaction. Instead, real civilization involves mastering our minds and passions by consciously and voluntarily restricting our wants. By limiting our material desires, Gandhi believes we can achieve true happiness, contentment, and moral elevation, which is the ultimate goal of human life.
(b) It shall be my privilege to contribute my share to this enchanting enterprise.
Answer:
This line is taken from Dr. Zakir Husain's speech, "Bharat is My Home," which he delivered after taking the oath as the President of India. In this context, the "enchanting enterprise" refers to the monumental and beautiful task of building a strong, prosperous, and culturally rich nation. Dr. Husain expresses his deep loyalty to India and its people. He humbly states that he considers it a great honor and privilege to serve the country and contribute whatever he can to the continuous growth, moral development, and welfare of the nation.

5. Answer any five of the following in about 40-50 words each:

(a) What did Mahatma Gandhi learn from modern civilization?
Ans. Mahatma Gandhi learned that we must shun modern civilization at all costs. He believed it is the worship of the material and the brute in us, which makes humanity lazy and a slave to greed, moving us away from moral elevation.
(d) How is the life of the Negro crippled ?
Ans. According to Martin Luther King Jr., the life of the Negro is crippled by the manacles of segregation and the chains of discrimination. Despite living in a vast ocean of material prosperity, they are forced to languish in poverty and social isolation.
(f) What is meant by 'Nature without check with original energy'?
Ans. In 'Song of Myself,' Walt Whitman uses this phrase to express his desire to live naturally and freely, without any restraints or prejudices. He wants to experience life with its raw, original, and purest energy, speaking his mind at every hazard.
(g) Who are the 'whispering neighbours'?
Ans. In the poem 'Now the Leaves are Falling Fast,' the 'whispering neighbours' refer to the messengers or angels of death. They are the forces that snatch away individuals from their real delight, isolating them from their loved ones as they approach the end of life.
